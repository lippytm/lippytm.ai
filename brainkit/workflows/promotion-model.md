# Promotion Model

This document defines how ideas, experiments, and repos should be promoted through the lippytm ecosystem.

The goal is to support **evolutionary engineering**: systems should be allowed to grow, but they should not jump from idea to production without structure.

---

## Promotion Stages

### Stage 0 — Concept
Early idea, note set, vision, or rough repo.

Required:
- purpose statement
- lane guess
- rough roadmap

### Stage 1 — Experiment
Isolated prototype or lab exploration.

Required:
- experiment objective
- success criteria
- sandbox or prototype separation
- clear labeling as experimental

### Stage 2 — Structured Prototype
A more repeatable form exists.

Required:
- architecture or product note
- setup path
- expected outputs
- declared risks

### Stage 3 — Managed Product Candidate
The system is useful enough for repeated internal or external use.

Required:
- repo role declared
- quality gates defined
- monetization or platform value declared
- integration boundaries documented
- operator procedures or runbooks begun

### Stage 4 — Production Product / Service
The system is stable enough for public, customer, or operational dependence.

Required:
- governance and review paths
- rollback awareness
- telemetry or logs
- release discipline
- policy alignment

### Stage 5 — Fleet Standard / Manufacturing Asset
The system is reusable as a template, pattern, or core manufacturing component.

Required:
- reusable structure
- strong docs
- repeatable rollout path
- propagation strategy
- downstream adoption potential

---

## Promotion Paths

Common patterns:

- `lab` → `product`
- `knowledge` → `revenue`
- `product` → `commerce`
- `swarm` → `control` support
- `hub` standards → cross-fleet adoption

Not every repo must reach Stage 5. Some should remain exploratory by design.

---

## Promotion Review Questions

Before promotion, ask:

1. what problem does this solve?
2. what lane does it belong to now?
3. what lane will it support after promotion?
4. what could break if it is promoted too early?
5. what quality evidence exists?
6. what commercial or platform value justifies the move?

---

## Best Practices

- promote by evidence, not enthusiasm alone
- preserve experimental energy in early stages
- document why a repo is graduating to a new stage
- move standards downstream only after proving usefulness
- let some repos stay permanently creative or exploratory when that is their job

---

## Rule of thumb

A good promotion model protects both innovation and structure by letting systems earn their next level of responsibility.
